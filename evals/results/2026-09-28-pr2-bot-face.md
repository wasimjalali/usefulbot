# PR 2: the new bot face and its motion, measured

Date: 2026-09-28. Branch `feat/bot-character`. Raw files: `2026-09-28-pr2-bot-face/`.

## Question

The bots got a new face (a circle with a smile) and a new working motion (the eyes show
what the bot is doing). Does the motion run at 60 fps, change pose without jumps, respect
Reduce motion, and keep CPU near what it was?

## What ran

- **Before:** the installed app built from `main` (efab1ff). The working face was eight PNG
  layers composited by a SwiftUI `TimelineView` capped at 30 fps.
- **After:** `macos/dist/Useful Bot.app` from this branch. The face is vector `CAShapeLayer`s
  and the loops are Core Animation keyframe animations, so no per-frame SwiftUI work.
- **Recordings:** `winrec` at 60 fps (1x), plus a scratch 2x build of it for the face detail.
  Every live turn went to Test Bot, except the one mistake noted below.
- **Harness:** `harness/run.sh` compiles the real `BotFaceLayer.swift` offscreen, switches
  poses, hops, turns Reduce motion on, and samples the live presentation transforms every
  1/60 s.
- **CPU:** `top -l 16 -s 2` on the app process, 30 s per window.
- **Cost:** no model spend beyond a few Test Bot turns.

## Numbers

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Face drawing rate | 30 fps cap; 34 fps recorded, 17 to 70 ms gaps | display rate; 56 fps recorded, 6 gaps over 34 ms in 24 s (the first is the AX walk that sends the message) |
| Idle CPU | 0.1 to 1.4% | 0.1 to 1.1% |
| CPU while thinking (same long chat) | 45 to 67% | 1 to 13% |
| CPU while streaming (same long chat) | 67 to 78% | 65 to 70% |

The first baseline (27% thinking, 47% streaming) was on a shorter chat. Both builds were
then measured on the same long Test Bot chat, which is the fair comparison. The raw series
are in `raw/*.txt`.

Harness, 36 pt face: the largest move in any 1/60 s frame is 0.26 pt, inside the writing
scan, not at a pose switch. Every switch eases in about 0.25 s (`raw/harness-pose-switches.txt`,
from an earlier version of `main.swift` that scripted think, tool, think, write, idle). The hop
lifted the head 6.1 pt at first; after review it lifts 4.7 pt (so a 36 pt rail row never
clips it), with squash 1.08/0.90 and stretch 0.95/1.06, then rests. With Reduce
motion on, nothing moves: the eyes hold the pose and the hop is skipped
(`raw/harness-hop-reduced.txt`).

## Bugs the measuring caught

1. **Double-height eyes.** An additive Core Animation scale adds to the resting 1, so each
   blink loop drew the eyes at 2x height. Only visible in the 2x stills (`images/pr2-hires-rail.png`);
   the 1x recording hid it. Scales are absolute now.
2. **Pose switches snapped.** Animations stamped with `CACurrentMediaTime()` as their begin
   time started "in the past" on this layer tree, so the 0.25 s blend was over before it
   drew. Letting Core Animation start them at commit fixed the eyes.
3. **The head tilt still snapped.** Reading `transform.rotation.z` by key path from the
   presentation layer returned nothing. Reading the angle off the matrix fixed it.
4. **Layers backed at 768 units.** The first version drew each shape at the SVG's size and
   scaled it down, which backs every layer at the big size. Paths are now scaled to points.
5. **A lost hop.** The rail face swapped view branches when a bot stopped working, which
   rebuilt it in the same update that bumped its hop counter.

## Mistake during the run

One test message ("run `sleep 4 && date`, then write about circles") went to Drive Admin
instead of Test Bot. A click meant to reselect Test Bot had failed silently after the
pinned-tile check. The turn was harmless and finished normally. It stays in that
conversation, because stopping it could have retired the session. After that, every send
first checked the header on a screenshot.

## Review pass 1: who caught what

SWE-2 high (Devin CLI, about 15 minutes) and one Opus 5.5 reviewer (about 4 minutes), split by
area. SWE-2 found the only high: a reply from a handoff or routine never marked the bot
"REPLY READY", because those turns have no local stream. Opus reviewed the drawing layer and
missed it (it wasn't in its area). SWE-2 also found two lows (a dead stream counted as a reply,
path numbers overflowing to infinity). Opus found six lows (hop plus blend lift, hop clipping,
two silent color parsers, two silent geometry fallbacks, dead avatar PNGs in the bundle,
Reduce motion not stopping a running hop). All fixed. The same evening the owner retired SWE-2
in Claude Code for an Opus plus Sonnet 5.5 pair, keeping SWE-2 for other harnesses; this pass
is the one on record where SWE-2's area held the high.

## Conclusion

The face draws at the display's rate, pose changes are continuous, and Reduce motion holds
it still. Thinking costs a fraction of what it did, and streaming is on par. What changed:
the face ships as described in `docs/plan/PR2-BOT-CHARACTER.md`.
